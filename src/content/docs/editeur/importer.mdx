You can start from an existing document: a PDF received by e-mail, a scanned
letter, or a photo of an invoice taken with a phone. The editor extracts its
text, which you then edit like any other document.

:::tip[Try it with our samples]
Download these fictional documents (in French) to follow the steps:
[PDF invoice](/editeur/exemples/facture-atelier-couture.pdf),
[scanned letter](/editeur/exemples/convocation-scannee.pdf),
[photo of a quote](/editeur/exemples/devis-photo.jpg).
:::

## 4.1 Import

1. Click **Importer** (Import) in the ribbon, then **Depuis l'ordinateur**
   (From the computer), or the **Importer** button in **Mes fichiers**.
2. Choose the file: **PDF**, **image** (JPG, PNG, TIFF, BMP, WebP) or text
   (HTML, TXT).
3. The document opens **as is**, in its original view.

The file is stored in the folder selected in **Mes fichiers**.

### With your phone

Got the document on paper? Photograph it with your phone, without logging in
on it: the pages go straight into the editor on your computer.

1. Click **Importer**, then **Avec mon téléphone (photo)** (With my phone),
   or the phone icon in **Mes fichiers**. A QR code appears.
2. Scan it with your phone's camera: a « Photographier un document »
   (Photograph a document) page opens.
3. Tap **Photographier une page** (Photograph a page) for each page (20 at
   most), or pick photos from the gallery. Remove a bad page with **×**.
4. Tap **Envoyer** (Send): on the computer, the document opens by itself.

Several pages make **one document**. It is then extracted like a scan
(section 4.2).

:::tip[For a readable photo]
Lay the page flat, well lit, with no glare or shadow from your hand, and
frame all of it.
:::

:::note[A single-use code]
The code is valid for **15 minutes** and works **only once**. For another
document, show a new code.
:::

## 4.2 Extract the text (the Modifier button)

Click **Modifier** (Edit — the pencil): the editor turns the document into
editable text, with its headings, lists and tables. What happens depends on
the document.

### A PDF that already contains text

This is the case for PDFs created on a computer (invoices, letters generated
by software). The text is extracted **instantly and for free**.

<Clip src="en/4-1-importer-un-pdf.mp4" caption="Importing a PDF invoice and extracting it" />

### A scanned document

A scan is only an image: artificial intelligence **reads each page**. Progress
is shown at the bottom of the screen (*Extraction en cours… page 1/2*). You can
keep working elsewhere or close the page: extraction carries on, and the text
will be waiting when you come back.

<Clip src="en/4-2-extraire-un-scan.mp4" caption="A two-page scanned letter (wait sped up)" />

### A photo taken with a phone

A photo is imported and extracted like a scan, **tables included**.

<Clip src="en/4-3-extraire-une-photo.mp4" caption="The photo of a quote, table included (wait sped up)" />

For a good result:

- photograph the sheet **flat**, well lit, with no shadow or glare;
- frame the whole page, and keep the phone's **full resolution** (avoid
  photos compressed by a messaging app);
- for a multi-page document, gather the pages into a single PDF before
  importing it, or import each page separately.

:::caution[Check amounts and references]
Reading a scan or a photo can go wrong, especially on a blurry or small photo:
one misread digit changes an amount. Compare every amount, date and number
(NINEA, invoice, ID document) with the original document.
:::

💳 Extracting a scan or a photo is charged by the number of words extracted,
once only: reopening the document afterwards is free. A document can have up
to 50 pages.

## 4.3 Check and correct

To compare with the original document, switch to **Aperçu** (Preview) and
choose the **Original** view in the status bar (see [part
5](/editeur/apercu/)). Then correct the text with the keyboard, by dictation
([part 2](/editeur/voix/)) or with Copilot ([part 3](/editeur/ia/)).

:::note[Images are not carried over yet]
Logos, stamps, signatures and photos in a scan are not carried over into the
extracted text. You can insert them again with **Insérer une image** (Insert
an image).
:::
