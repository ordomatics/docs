A project can serve more than one business. Each client you onboard gets their own Odoo
database and their own address, running on the servers your project already has. They sign in
at the address you hand them; running it stays with you.

<StepVideo dir="manage" src="onboard-a-client.mp4" caption="Onboarding a client, start to finish. The waiting is played fast; the video has no sound." />

## Before you start

You need a project that is **Ready**, with its production environment running. If you do not
have one yet, follow [Getting Started](/start/getting-started/).

## 1. Open the production environment

In the portal, open **Projects**, then your project, then **Edit** next to its production
environment. The **Clients** list on that page starts with one line: your project's own Odoo,
marked **you**.

## 2. Enter the client's name and a contact email

Under the list, type the client's name and a contact email, then press **Onboard**.

The name decides the address. A client called `Baobab` on the project `ged` gets
`https://baobab.ged.ordomatics.com`.

## 3. Wait for it to be ready

The client appears in the list straight away, and its status moves through **Setting up** to
**Ready** in about four minutes. That is the platform creating the database and installing the
same modules your project runs.

The address is published just after that, by a redeploy of your servers. Allow a few more
minutes before you open it.

:::caution
The first client you onboard restarts all of your project's servers once, so your own Odoo is
unavailable for a few minutes while that happens. Pick a quiet moment. Later clients
only restart the background jobs.
:::

## 4. Hand over the address

Open the client's address and sign in. A new database starts with the login `admin` and the
password `admin`: change that password before you give the address to anyone.

## Afterwards

Each client has its own line in the **Clients** list:

- **Pause** stops serving that client without touching their data. **Resume** brings them back.
- **Auto-upgrade** decides whether their database is upgraded with each release of your
  project. It is on by default.
- **Offboard** removes the client and their database. You confirm by typing your project's
  name.

Every client shares your project's servers, so each one you add uses some of their memory.
